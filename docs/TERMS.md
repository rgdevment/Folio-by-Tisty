# Folio by Tisty terms of use

**Version 7, in force since November 6, 2026.**

## The short version

Folio turns a Tisty document into a link for sharing it. It has no accounts: the
link is the permission. What you share is encrypted, and the service keeps it
without the key to read it. When reporting, whoever has the link can hand it to
the operator, and the copy can stay for up to 180 days even if you stop sharing.
It is free, non-profit and without warranties: it can fail, change or end. Your
document always stays in your Tisty; the link is a copy that expires.

## Who runs it

Folio is run by Mario, a natural person in Chile, as a non-profit project. There
is no company behind it and nobody makes money from it, one person looks after

<abuso@tisty.app>. The service's code is public, under the [Functional Source

## What Folio is

Folio is a service apart from the Tisty app. Tisty writes, signs and encrypts;
Folio keeps what arrives and hands it to whoever has the link. Folio only
receives and shows: documents are edited in Tisty, never here.

## What they cover

## Who can use it

You must be at least 14. If you are under 18, you share with the permission of
whoever looks after you. Reading a link asks for no account and no acceptance,
but what you do with what you read is your responsibility.

## How it works

- **Sharing happens only from Tisty**, and what is shared appears in no list:
  whoever has the link reads it.
- **Each document has three links:** one to read, one to edit and one to manage.
  Whoever has a link has that power; look after them like a key. A link to read
  cannot be taken back unless you stop sharing.
- **What is shared is encrypted end to end.** The key travels in the link, after
  the `#`, and browsers do not send it to the server. The server keeps bytes it
  cannot read. If you lose the link, nobody can recover it for you.
- **What is shared expires on its own** after the time without activity you
  choose when sharing, from one day to 15 at most: no reads, no visits of yours
  and no new versions. When it expires, the links stop working and the server's
  copy is deleted. Your document stays in Tisty and you can share it again.
- **Stopping sharing** cuts the links at once. The copy is deleted at the next
  cleanup, normally within an hour. A report can prevent that deletion, and you
  will not be told.
- **There are limits** on size, pictures and attachments, documents shared at
  once and uploads per hour, which the service can change. Today each key may
  have two documents shared at once. Tisty warns you before sharing if something
  does not fit.
- **If the service reaches its spending limit,** it may stop taking documents or
  answering for a while. While it is stopped, nothing expires.

## Your key and your reputation

- **You accept these terms** the first time you share from Tisty. Before you
  accept, Tisty shows you all of them, and you can open them on the web to save
  or print them. What you sign says which version you accepted, and Folio keeps
  that version and the hour beside your key. Without it, Folio does not take
  what you upload. If you use another program, signing with your key is
  accepting them, even if it does not show them to you.
- **Whoever uploads a version with an edit link** also accepts these terms, and
  their key is kept like the owner's.
- **Your key is how Folio tells you apart.** Tisty signs what you share with a
  key of yours, and Folio keeps its public part for good, even when your
  documents are gone, unless you ask to have it deleted as the [privacy
  policy](PRIVACY.md) says. It does not say who you are. The name you choose to
  show travels encrypted, and only whoever has the link reads it.
- **Your reputation belongs to that key.** Limits start low, and reputation is
  what will give access to more. How it is earned is not decided yet, and these
  terms will say when it is. Reports alone never take it away: only what the
  operator confirms counts.
- **A new key starts from the lowest limits,** and each network can bring only a
  few new ones a day.

## What is not allowed

Even though nobody can read what is encrypted, sharing it is still your
responsibility. Folio may not be used for:

- Child sexual abuse material, in any form.
- Sexually explicit images, of anyone.
- Intimate images of someone without their consent.
- Phishing, malware, scams or deceit to steal data or money.
- Threats, or concrete incitement to commit a crime.
- Publishing someone's personal data to harass or expose them.
- Anything else that is a crime in Chile.
- Content that infringes copyright, when its holder claims it.
- Attacking the service, flooding it or getting around its limits.

## Reports and removal

In this section, a **network** is the connection a report comes from, and a
**provider** is the company that gives that connection. Folio does not ask who
you are to report: it keeps a digest of the network.

- **Anyone with the link can report** from the page where it is read. Each
  network reports once per document, and Folio asks its browser for a small
  piece of computing work to slow abuse. Whoever does not have the link can
  write to <abuso@tisty.app> or go to the PDI or the prosecutor.
- **A light report** (spam, or something annoying or offensive that is no crime)
  needs nobody to step in. With three networks from at least two providers,
  whoever shared it sees a warning in their manage link, also from Tisty, and
  has three days to upload a new version. If they do not, it stops being served
  until one arrives. A new version takes those reports back to zero.
- **A report of deceit** (phishing, malware or a scam) may bring the link to
  read it, if the reporter agrees. With three networks from at least two
  providers, the document is blocked: it stops being served, but is not deleted.
- **A grave report** (threats or violence, concrete incitement, personal data
  published to harm, intimate images or child sexual abuse) always brings the
  link to read it, encrypted so only the operator can open it, and never the
  part that allows editing. It blocks with the same three networks, or when the
  operator decides.
- **A single report of deceit or a grave one keeps the document:** if it expires
  or you stop sharing it, it is not served, but its copy stays for up to 180
  days from the report, in case an authority asks for it. An order can ask for
  more.
- **A false report is discarded.** If the link it brings does not open the
  document, or the operator finds it false, the operator discards it, and
  reports from that network stop counting on every document for up to 180 days.
  Until then it counts like any other. If an appeal is accepted, the reports on
  that version stop counting, but a new version can be reported.
- **You may appeal once per document** in your manage link. If every version
  reported was signed by someone you let edit, and there is no report of child
  abuse, the appeal is accepted on its own: the edit link stops working and it
  stays blocked until you upload a version of your own. Other appeals go to the
  operator, who accepts or rejects them with no duty to explain why. You may
  write to <abuso@tisty.app>.
- **The operator may remove** any document that breaks these terms, and block
  access to whoever abuses the service. What the operator blocks only the
  operator lifts, no new version does, and the copy stays for up to 180 days.
- **The operator may report** to the PDI or the prosecutor what, on review,
  looks like a crime, and tell Google Safe Browsing and Cloudflare about
  phishing and malware. One person does this, by hand.
- **The operator only opens what is encrypted** in four cases and always with
  the link to read it: when whoever shared it hands it over to show the block
  was a mistake, to review a grave report with the link it brought, to confirm
  phishing or malware before reporting it, or under a judge's order. Every
  opening is noted. In a browser, the code that decrypts comes from the same
  server; the [privacy policy](PRIVACY.md) says what that does not protect
  against.
- **A report of child abuse is reviewed by a person,** who opens the document
  only to verify it, without malice or dishonest intent, and may not keep a copy
  on their device. If it is false, it is discarded. If not, that person blocks the
  document and reports it to the relevant authorities, the only ones who get the
  key, along with the records Folio holds. The copy stays encrypted in Folio for
  up to 180 days, or as long as an order asks. Whoever reports must not download,
  copy, forward or email that material.
- **Copyright is claimed by its holder** at <abuso@tisty.app>. A compulsory
  removal takes a court order, as Ley 17.336 says, and the operator may also act
  on a DMCA notice. The operator may stop taking uploads from a repeat
  infringer, when a court finds so. A knowingly false notice has legal
  consequences.
- **Authorities write** to <abuso@tisty.app>. An order may require keeping a
  document and its records beyond these periods. The operator complies with what
  the law requires and does not contest it on your behalf. Without the link,
  what is encrypted stays unreadable to the operator too.

## Public mode (does not exist yet)

Nothing in this section works today, and accepting these terms does not bind you
to it. It says what is planned, which may change. Before you use it, Tisty will
ask you to accept its rules separately.

- **What is published would not be encrypted.** It would be kept signed and
  unencrypted, and anyone could read it at folio.tisty.app, the operator too.
  The server would neither open nor change it, and would serve it from a cache.
- **It would not expire for lack of readers.** It would be removed after 12
  months with no activity of yours, with notice in Tisty, or when you delete it.
  A copy in a cache, a search engine or the hands of whoever read it could go on
  existing.
- **It would start out of search engines** until it shows signs of being
  legitimate.
- **You would still be its author.** Publishing would give the operator
  permission to keep it, show it and cache it, without payment, while it stays
  published.
- **Also forbidden would be** racism and the glorification of crime.
- **It would be blocked** by reports from 10 networks of at least three
  providers, or from 1% of its readers if that is more, with the operator's
  review.
- **Comments and votes** could come later. Whoever writes them would answer for
  them.
